## Overview

Kalakriti is an immersive 3D painting experience inspired by **Kalamkari**, an ancient Indian art form traditionally created with a tamarind pen and natural dyes. Rather than recreating the practice as a flat digital canvas, the project invites people to step into a spatial environment and tell their own stories through movement and drawing.

Built with A-Painter and A-Frame, the experience explores how virtual reality can extend a traditional visual language while preserving the qualities that make it distinctive: organic strokes, recurring natural motifs, and a restrained, material-led colour palette.

## A forest as the canvas

The environment is framed as a Kalamkari forest, drawing on motifs such as flowers, leaves, peacocks, and trees. Traditional Kalamkari also depicts stories from epics including the *Ramayana* and *Mahabharata* across objects, apparel, and canvas. Kalakriti shifts this storytelling into an explorable world where the whole environment becomes the surface for expression.

One guided activity centres on a bare tree. A reference painting introduces the free-flowing, organic linework characteristic of Kalamkari, then invites visitors to create their own leaves. The goal is not to reproduce a flat image, but to use body movement and depth to extend a stroke across planes in three dimensions.

## Interaction design

Visitors can move through the scene, paint with either controller trigger, and open colour palettes to select tools and colours. The palette is intentionally limited to hues that echo Kalamkari's natural dyes, reinforcing the art form's organic and nature-conscious character while giving the experience a coherent visual language.

At the end of the activity, visitors can capture their painted tree through the Oculus menu and share it, turning a personal VR interaction into a take-away artifact.

## Cultural heritage in immersive media

Kalakriti was informed by work on immersive technology as a medium for cultural learning and heritage engagement. Gaitatzes et al. explored virtual reality for revisiting historical culture, while later work on WebAR and Cantonese porcelain examined how interactive 3D experiences can support traditional handicraft learning. Together, these studies helped frame VR not simply as a display medium, but as a space for active participation and creative interpretation.

- [Reviving the past: cultural heritage meets virtual reality](https://doi.org/10.1145/584993.585011)
- [Exploring Traditional Handicraft Learning Mode using WebAR Technology](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3309700.3338453)
- [AR, VR and 3D printing for co-creation in cultural heritage visitor experiences](https://www.emerald.com/insight/content/doi/10.1108/JPMD-07-2016-0045/full/html?journalCode=jpmd)
