## Overview

Video calls depend on visual cues that can become harder to interpret as latency and jitter increase. This CHI ’22 study examined how those network conditions affect a visual communication task in Zoom, helping explain a contributor to video-conferencing fatigue.

## Study design

The study recruited 18 participants for four rounds of Charades. An actor silently enacted movie titles while participants guessed them over a video call. Each successive round introduced more network degradation, from no injected latency to 800 ms latency with 30% packet loss.

The study collected task-completion time, correct guesses, NASA-TLX workload ratings, and post-task interviews. The supplied diagram shows the experimental setup: a moderator coordinated the session while latency and jitter were injected between the actor and participant.

## Findings

Participants could adapt to latency and jitter while playing the engaging visual task, and the study found no significant loss in task performance across rounds. Mental workload and frustration increased as the network degraded, however, with 14 of 18 participants reporting concerns or annoyance about latency by the final task.

## Publication

Published as “A Study of the Effects of Network Latency on Visual Task Performance in Video Conferencing” in the *CHI Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems Extended Abstracts* (2022), with Ayushi Srivastava, Mashhuda Glencross, and Ojaswa Sharma.
